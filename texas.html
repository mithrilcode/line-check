If a cooling check fails

Missed 70°F in 2 hours: reheat to 165°F and restart cooling, or discard. Missed 41°F at 6 hours: discard.

Exclude or restrict staff

  • Exclude anyone with vomiting or diarrhea until 24 hours symptom-free
  • Exclude for jaundice, or a diagnosis of norovirus, hepatitis A, Shigella, Shiga toxin E. coli, Salmonella Typhi or non-typhoidal Salmonella
  • Restrict sore throat with fever, and cover infected cuts or burns on hands
  • Notify your local health department when an employee is diagnosed with one of those illnesses

What can close you

An imminent health hazard lets the health authority suspend your permit on the spot. Stop operating and call them yourself if you have:

  • No hot water, or no potable water
  • Sewage backing up
  • A rodent or insect infestation
  • A power outage long enough to lose refrigeration
  • Fire or flood damage

Agencies

  • Food permit & inspections: your city or county health department (Houston, Dallas, Austin/Travis County, San Antonio Metro Health and others). Where none exists, Texas DSHS.
  • Alcohol: Texas Alcoholic Beverage Commission
  • Sales, mixed beverage & franchise tax: Texas Comptroller
  • Workers' comp: TDI Division of Workers' Compensation
  • Employment notices: Texas Workforce Commission
  • Hood, suppression, extinguishers: your city fire marshal; contractors are licensed by the State Fire Marshal's Office
Limits come from the Texas Food Establishment Rules (25 TAC Chapter 228), which most city and county health departments adopt and sometimes add to. Your local health department sets permit terms, fees and inspection scoring. Confirm anything you're unsure about with them. This tool helps you keep records; it isn't legal or tax advice.
